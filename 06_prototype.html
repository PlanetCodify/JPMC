Trust Platform/Overview

Overview

A firmwide gen-AI governance layer, piloted in International Consumer Bank (ICB) — classifies gen-AI and agentic features by risk, auto-clears the low-risk ones, and routes the highest-risk cases into JPMC's existing Model Risk Governance & Review (MRGR) process instead of standing up a new governance function next to it.

MRGR's authority isn't ICB-specific — the evidence for its current scope just happens to come from a job posting scoped to ICB, and appears to cover traditional statistical models only, the same boundary the Fed's SR 26-2 guidance draws for gen-AI firmwide. This platform is the technical layer that extends MRGR's existing authority into the category it doesn't cover yet. It doesn't compete with MRGR, it feeds it — from any JPMC line of business, not just ICB.

Why this exists now

On April 17, 2026, the Federal Reserve issued SR 26-2, superseding SR 11-7, and explicitly excluded generative and agentic AI from formal model-risk scope for banks over $30B in assets — firmwide, for JPMC as a whole. A live MRGR job posting scoped to ICB lists only traditional quantitative-model skills, with no mention of gen-AI, LLMs, or agentic systems. Read together: MRGR almost certainly has the standing and expertise to govern this, but no confirmed entry point for it yet, in any line of business. That gap is what this platform closes — not by inventing a new governance body, but by building the triage layer that decides what needs MRGR's attention and routes it there automatically. See the Governance Simulator to try the classifier, or MRGR Queue to see what a reviewer actually sees.

What changed from the original pitch

Worth knowing if you're comparing this to an earlier version The first draft of this platform proposed a full customer data, communications, and CMS platform, with AI governance as one feature bundled inside it. A 7-perspective review panel flagged that as backwards — it bet the largest investment on the least-validated assumption (whether Risk & Compliance even wants standardized tiering). This version ships the governance layer alone first, validates it against a real function (MRGR), and defers the data/comms/CMS build to a later phase, decided only if the governance layer proves itself.
Grounded entirely in public sources (Federal Reserve, JPMC press coverage, ICB public disclosures, a public MRGR job posting). Makes no claim to JPMorgan Chase's actual internal systems, vendors, or roadmap — see the accompanying PRD and research notes for the full source trail.

Architecture

Everything inside the platform boundary below is what gets built. MRGR's own review process sits outside it on purpose — this platform calls into it, it doesn't redesign it.

Tier 1 — low risk, synchronous
Tier 2 — medium risk, synchronous + sampled
Tier 3 — high risk, async escalation
External — already exists at JPMC

The three decisions that matter

Not every design choice is worth calling out. These three are, because getting any one of them wrong changes what the platform actually is.

DECISION 01
Sync only where staleness is dangerous
Tier 1 and Tier 2 both resolve synchronously so the engineer never waits on a human. The one place this platform deliberately breaks that pattern is the audit trail write for Tier 2/3 — those are logged async, since a delayed audit entry is a much smaller risk than a delayed classification response.
DECISION 02
Version the platform's own API
Market teams integrate against this platform's contract, not against MRGR's internal systems directly. If MRGR's own tooling changes, or a future CDP/CMS vendor gets selected in Phase 2+, that change happens behind the API and is invisible to teams already integrated.
DECISION 03
Governance is a callable service, not a gate
A market team calls the classifier the same way it would call any other platform service — there's no separate "governance review request" process bolted on top. Tier 3 results route into MRGR's real queue, not a mock approval step this platform invents on its own.

The three tiers

The tier is a deterministic function of two fields: whether it touches a credit/account decision, and whether it touches any customer data source. A classifier outage still defaults every in-flight request to Tier 2, never silently to Tier 1 — a named requirement in the PRD's AI Behavior Contract, not an implementation detail.

TierRuleReview pathExample
Tier 1credit_or_account_decision=false and data_sources=[]Automated policy check, no human reviewContent copy variants, translations
Tier 2credit_or_account_decision=false and data_sources.length>0Automated check + logged, periodic human spot-checkNext-best-action messaging
Tier 3credit_or_account_decision=trueFull human-in-the-loop, routed to MRGRCredit decision narratives

Why this view exists The PRD flags that a "consistent, defensible review process" is meaningless without a real reviewer-facing surface — the first review panel caught that this was implied but never designed. This is that surface, at a stub level: a queue, a stated SLA, and full audit-trail visibility before any decision is made.
