Executive Summary

Problem
In April 2026 the Federal Reserve excluded generative and agentic AI from formal bank model-risk rules (SR 26-2) — firmwide, for JPMC as a whole. JPMC already runs a review function with exactly this kind of authority — Model Risk Governance & Review (MRGR) — evidenced through a job posting scoped to International Consumer Bank (ICB), but its public scope stops at traditional statistical models. The gap isn't that nobody can govern this. It's that nobody built the way in, in any line of business.
Proposed solution
A firmwide automated tiering layer that classifies gen-AI and agentic features by risk, clears low-risk ones on its own, and escalates the rest into MRGR's real queue — with the reasoning and audit trail already attached. Not a new governance function; the missing entry point into one that already exists. Piloted in ICB first, where the regulatory pressure and the evidence for MRGR's existing scope both happen to be strongest.
Business impact
Closes a live, dated regulatory gap firmwide without duplicating MRGR's expertise or regulatory relationships · lets ICB, the pilot line of business, ship gen-AI features at competitive pace against UK peers already live with AI-driven experiences (NatWest's Cora, Lloyds' Envoy) · likely lower build cost than standing up a parallel review function, and reusable by other JPMC lines of business once proven

Problem Statement

International Consumer Bank is scaling a branchless, digital-only retail model across markets — UK live since 2021, Germany publicly targeted for 2026. Every new market ships more gen-AI-adjacent features, right as a real regulatory gap opened underneath all of them.

On April 17, 2026, the Federal Reserve issued SR 26-2, superseding SR 11-7, and explicitly excluded generative and agentic AI from formal model-risk scope for banks over $30B in assets — JPMC clears that threshold many times over. Separately, a live MRGR job posting scoped to ICB lists only traditional quantitative-model skills: financial math, probability theory, numerical methods. No mention of gen-AI, LLMs, or agentic systems anywhere in it.

Read together, those two facts say the same thing from opposite directions: the expertise to govern this exists. The entry point doesn't.

Who
ICB market launch engineers, JPMC's Model Risk Governance & Review (MRGR) function, and ICB digital banking customers
Root cause
The obvious fix — a new governance function for gen-AI — is also the wrong one. MRGR already has the regulatory relationships and review discipline this needs; a parallel function would duplicate what's already there and add a second, less-trusted process to maintain.
Compounding constraint
Research on comparable internal-platform populations shows 64% of engineers bypass a platform that's slower than building the workaround themselves. A governance layer that's technically correct but slow to integrate against doesn't get used — it gets routed around, and the SR 26-2 gap stays exactly as open as before anyone built anything.
Impact of inaction
Every gen-AI-adjacent feature ships with no defensible review path by default; UK peers (NatWest, Lloyds) are already live with AI-driven experiences, raising the competitive floor while the gap stays open

Solution Overview

What we're building — a tiering layer that classifies a gen-AI or agentic feature request into one of three risk tiers and returns the required review path. Tier 1 clears an automated policy check. Tier 2 gets an automated check plus a logged human spot-check. Tier 3 routes into MRGR's existing review workflow, with the classifier's full reasoning and an audit trail attached. Built as firmwide infrastructure from the start — versioned API, no ICB-specific assumptions baked into the contract — but launched with one pilot line of business so the riskiest assumption (will MRGR actually use this) gets tested before any other JPMC line of business adopts it.

How the system actually behaves

The mechanics behind the proposal — what the classifier is required to do, how the architecture is structured, and what a reviewer actually sees.

Requirements — Behavior Specification

Task — classify a gen-AI or agentic feature request into one of three risk tiers and return the required review path, routing Tier 3 into MRGR's real queue rather than a parallel process this platform invents.

Inputs
Feature description, data sources touched, customer-facing surface, whether it influences a credit or account decision
Constraints
Never auto-approves anything touching a credit or account decision. Self-routing logic for credit-adjacent features requires a fair-lending (ECOA/Reg B) review before this ships — the classifier doesn't get to decide its own jurisdiction over that category.
Disallowed
Silent approval of anything the classifier is uncertain about — ambiguous input defaults to Tier 2, never Tier 1. No fail-safe if the classifier itself is unavailable also defaults to Tier 2, logged, never a silent failure.
Latency budget
P50 under 2 seconds, P95 under 10 seconds — synchronous enough that an engineer never files a ticket and waits

System Architecture

Everything inside the boundary is what gets built. MRGR's own review process sits outside it on purpose — this platform calls into it, it doesn't redesign it.

The one deliberate exception to an otherwise synchronous design: Tier 2 and Tier 3 both write to the same Audit Trail Store, so a rollback decision has one place to check, not two.

Compliance & ethics constraints

Default-deny, not default-allow.Ambiguous classification and classifier downtime both default to Tier 2, never silently to Tier 1 — the system escalates its own uncertainty rather than hiding it.
No self-governance on credit decisions.The classifier can never auto-approve a feature touching a credit or account decision, under any circumstance. That category always reaches a human, by construction.
The integrator and reviewer experiences were assumed, never designed — until the review caught it.The adoption thesis rested on an integration path that was never specified. The prototype's MRGR Queue tab and documented sandbox exist specifically because this was flagged as missing, not nice-to-have.

Rollout Plan

Governance ships standalone in Phase 1, not bundled with a customer data/comms/CMS build — reversed from the original plan after review found it bet the largest investment on the least-validated assumption in the thesis.

Open Questions

01
Does MRGR's actual scope for ICB already cover gen-AI, contrary to what one job posting suggests? Checkable, not a guess, for anyone with access to ask — the single highest-priority question in the whole platform.
02
Would MRGR want an automated triage layer feeding into its process, or see it as unnecessary?
03
Does the existing model-risk process actually have scope to cover gen-AI outputs feeding credit decisions, once Tier 3 routes there?
04
UK-first or Germany-first sequencing? NatWest's Cora and Lloyds' Envoy are already live in the UK market Chase competes in — deliberately left unresolved here, not silently decided.
05
Does JPMC's engineering culture (MACH Alliance membership, existing LLM Suite platform) mean the general 64% platform-bypass benchmark doesn't fully transfer here?
06
Is there a ValidMind-class tool already inside JPMC's model-risk stack that Tier 3 should call into?

Assumptions Made

01
MRGR's public job posting accurately reflects its current scope — one source, not a policy document, and could lag reality
02
No existing internal JPMC CDP, CMS, or comms vendor was found publicly — treated as a genuine unknown, not evidence of absence
03
Germany is the likely first pilot market given the active public launch timeline, though UK-first is left as an explicitly open question
04
The general 64% platform-bypass adoption statistic is a reasonable prior for JPMC's engineering culture, flagged in Open Questions as possibly not fully transferable
